# Arabic-Language On-Device/Local AI Landscape (as of 2026-11-25)

## Key Question 1: What are the most capable small (<8B params) open-weight models with good Arabic/Egyptian-dialect support released or significantly updated recently?

### Takeaway
Nile-Chat (MBZUAI-Paris, dense 4B/12B + 3x4B-A6B MoE, built on Gemma-3) is the standout Egyptian-Arabic-specific small-model release, beating Jais, ALLaM, and Llama-3.1-8B on Egyptian-script benchmarks; ALLaM-7B-Instruct and Fanar-1-9B-Instruct are the next-strongest sub-10B general Arabic options with partial dialectal strength, while Qwen3's small sizes (4B/8B) show a measurable Arabic-specific accuracy drop and are not dialect-specialized.

### Cited Findings
- **Nile-Chat family** (MBZUAI-Paris, arXiv:2507.04569, posted 6 Jul 2025): dense **Nile-Chat-4B** and **Nile-Chat-12B**, plus MoE variants **Nile-Chat-3x4B-A6B** (3 experts, 2 active, ~6B activated params) and **Nile-Chat-2x4B-A6B**. Dense models are built from Gemma-3 bases; the MoE uses Branch-Train-MiX (BTX) merging an Arabic-script expert, a Latin-script (Arabizi) expert, and the base model. Designed to handle Egyptian Arabic in both Arabic and Latin (Arabizi) script. — [arXiv:2507.04569](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04569v1)
- Nile-Chat-12B benchmark scores (Egyptian-script tasks, self-reported): Egyptian MMLU 62.59, Egyptian HellaSwag 64.04, Egyptian PIQA 70.69, Egyptian AlpacaEval 95.56 — highest of all compared models on every Arabic-script discriminative task tested. Nile-Chat-4B: MMLU 50.25, HellaSwag 55.92, PIQA 67.30, AlpacaEval 86.95. Comparators: ALLaM-7B-Instruct-preview (MMLU 60.08, HellaSwag 57.29, PIQA 66.10, AlpacaEval 69.55), Jais-family-13b-chat (44.85/52.99/64.85/52.52), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (42.88/43.10/57.97/52.35). — [arXiv:2507.04569](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04569v1)
- A related/earlier paper, **NileChat** (arXiv:2505.18383), describes a **3B model** covering both Egyptian and Moroccan dialectal Arabic in Arabic script and Arabizi — the smallest model in this family, relevant for phone deployment. — [arXiv:2505.18383](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18383v3)
- Model/dataset/eval-code licensing for Nile-Chat: the paper states "all resources are publicly available," but the only explicit license visible on the arXiv page is CC BY 4.0 for the paper itself — the model weights' license was **not confirmed** in available text (must check the HuggingFace model card directly before relying on Apache/MIT-style redistribution). — [arXiv:2507.04569](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04569v1)
- Independent benchmark cross-check (DialectalArabicMMLU, arXiv:2510.27543, posted 31 Oct 2025) on 5 dialects including Egyptian, evaluating 19 open-weight models (1.6B–13B params): dialect-average accuracy — Nile-Chat-12B 59.9%, gemma-3-12b-it 58.7%, Falcon-H1-7B-Instruct 57.2%, Fanar-1-9B-Instruct 56.2%, ALLaM-7B-Instruct 56.0%; among ~3B models, NileChat-3B scored 52.7%. — [arXiv:2510.27543](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.27543v1)
- **ALLaM** (Saudi SDAIA/HUMAIN): ALLaM-7B-Instruct-preview and a newer **ALLaM-2-7b-instruct** are available on Hugging Face/Azure AI catalog. ALLaM 1 models are built by continuing pretraining on Llama 2 base models; the ALLaM 1 license is a SDAIA-issued agreement requiring redistribution to also pass along the original Llama 2 Community License (i.e., **not** a clean Apache/MIT license — it is Llama-2-license-encumbered). — [IBM ALLaM 1 License PDF](https://ibm.com/docs/en/SSYOK8/wsj/analyze-data/assets/ALLaM_1_License.pdf); [HF ALLaM-7B-Instruct-preview](https://huggingface.co/ALLaM-AI/ALLaM-7B-Instruct-preview); [arXiv:2407.15390](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.15390v1)
- **Fanar** (Qatar Computing Research Institute / QCRI): core models are **Fanar Star** and **Fanar Prime**; a 2025 Nature Middle East piece reports the team focused on smaller models in the **7B and 9B parameter** range. A newer paper, **Fanar 2.0** (arXiv:2603.16397, ~March 2026 per ID), reports "substantial benchmark improvements" over Fanar 1.0, but exact parameter counts/license for 2.0 could not be extracted from the fetched PDF (binary/compressed, details not retrievable in this session). — [Nature Middle East](https://www.natureasia.com/en/nmiddleeast/article/10.1038/nmiddleeast.2025.142); [QCRI Fanar](https://qcai.qcri.org/?p=3192); [arXiv:2603.16397](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.16397)
- **Jais** (G42/Inception/Cerebras/MBZUAI) lineage: original Jais-13B open-sourced Aug 2023 (built on/adapted from Llama 2-style architecture choices at larger sizes e.g. Jais-30B/70B). A newer **"Jais 2"** (70B, described as trained on "richest Arabic-first dataset," available on Hugging Face) and an even newer **"Jais 3: Introducing Arabic-centric Agentic AI"** (arXiv:2611.10224, dated ~Nov 2026) exist, but exact small-model parameter counts, license terms, and Egyptian-dialect benchmark comparisons for Jais 2/3 could **not** be confirmed from the fetched PDFs in this session (text was compressed/unreadable via the fetch tool). On the older Jais-family-13b-chat, DialectalArabicMMLU/Nile-Chat tables show it trailing Nile-Chat, ALLaM, and Fanar on Egyptian tasks (see scores above). — [arXiv:2611.10224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10224v2); [MBZUAI Jais 2 announcement](https://mbzuai.ac.ae/news/inception-cerebras-and-mbzuai-release-jais-2-the-next-generation-of-the-worlds-leading-arabic-open-weight-llm/)
- **Qwen3 small sizes on Arabic:** In a tool-calling benchmark, qwen3-4b accuracy drops from up to 100% on English prompts to up to 82% on Arabic prompts (same tasks). In the Jais 2 paper's comparison table, Qwen3-8B (non-thinking mode) averaged 30.92 across four Arabic knowledge benchmarks, trailing gemma-2-9b-it (34.30). Qwen3 is not Egyptian/dialect-specialized — it is a general multilingual model. Larger Qwen3 (235B-A22B) leads open-weight models on Stanford HELM Arabic (mean 0.786), but that is far too large for on-device use. — [arXiv tool-calling benchmark](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.05101); [Jais 2 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13580); [HELM Arabic](https://crfm.stanford.edu/2025/12/18/helm-arabic.html)
- **SILMA** appears as an "open-weights Arabic-specific" model family on the Stanford HELM Arabic leaderboard alongside AceGPT-v2, ALLaM, and JAIS, but parameter sizes were not given on the page — worth a direct follow-up if evaluating candidates further. — [HELM Arabic](https://crfm.stanford.edu/2025/12/18/helm-arabic.html)

### Inferences
- For ARA's on-device Egyptian-Arabic use case, **Nile-Chat-3B/4B** (and the 3x4B-A6B MoE if llama.cpp MoE support is adequate) is currently the best-evidenced candidate: it's the only model family specifically benchmarked and purpose-built for Egyptian Arabic (not just MSA) at a phone-viable size, and it beats larger general Arabic models (Jais-13B, ALLaM-7B) on Egyptian-specific tasks in two independent evaluations (its own paper and the independent DialectalArabicMMLU paper).
- ALLaM-7B and Fanar-1-9B are reasonable fallbacks for general Arabic (MSA) quality but are not dialect-specialized and ALLaM's license carries Llama-2-license obligations, which may matter for ARA's "free-first" distribution model.
- Qwen's small models should not be assumed "good at Arabic" by virtue of being popular/well-supported in llama.cpp; they show real degradation specifically on Arabic, and no evidence of Egyptian-dialect tuning.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm the **license** for Nile-Chat model weights (only the paper's CC BY 4.0 was visible) — needs a direct check of the Hugging Face model card.
- Could not extract **Jais 2 / Jais 3** parameter counts for any small (<8B) variants, license terms, or their Egyptian-dialect scores — PDF text was not extractable via the fetch tool in this session; the HTML/abstract versions or Hugging Face model cards should be checked directly.
- Could not extract **Fanar 2.0** parameter counts or license — same PDF extraction limitation.
- Did not verify **GGUF availability / llama.cpp compatibility** for any of these models directly (no model card for quantized formats was checked); Gemma-3-based architectures (Nile-Chat dense variants) are very likely to have llama.cpp/GGUF support given Gemma-3's mainline support, but this was not directly confirmed in this research pass.
- SILMA's parameter size/license was not determined.

## Key Question 2: Are there openly licensed (Apache-2.0/MIT) small embedding models with strong Arabic cross-lingual retrieval quality, suitable for on-device semantic search?

### Takeaway
Several Apache-2.0 Arabic/multilingual embedding models exist on Hugging Face at phone-viable sizes (0.1B–4B), but most lack confirmed dialectal (vs. MSA-only) evaluation, and the most rigorous published family (Swan/ArabicMTEB) is a research release whose license was not confirmed as permissive.

### Cited Findings
- **bahith-arabic-retrieval-4b**: fine-tuned from Qwen3-Embedding-4B, license **apache-2.0** (inherited from base model), reports nDCG@10 of 0.7971 at 2560 dimensions; card states training is primarily on MSA with limited dialectal coverage. — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/mabdulaziz499/bahith-arabic-retrieval-4b)
- **arabic-gte-multilingual-embed-medium**: built on Alibaba's gte-multilingual-base, 768-dim output, 8192 token max sequence length, license **Apache-2.0**. — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/RamzyBakir/arabic-gte-multilingual-embed-medium)
- **Arabic-Retrieval-v1.0** (omarelshehy): Sentence-Transformer, BertModel + mean pooling, fine-tuned from aubmindlab/bert-base-arabertv02; **0.1B params** (F32 safetensors), 768-dim embeddings, max seq length 512 tokens, license **apache-2.0**. Benchmarks: MTEB MIRACLRetrieval (Arabic) NDCG@10 = 58.664 (self-reported); on three internal eval sets, NDCG@10 of 0.875/0.72/0.679 vs. intfloat/multilingual-e5-large's 0.89/0.719/0.698 (roughly comparable, slightly behind on 2 of 3, ahead on 1 of 3). Card claims dialect sensitivity but names no specific dialects and gives no dialect-specific numbers. — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/omarelshehy/Arabic-Retrieval-v1.0)
- **SA-STS-Embeddings-0.2B** (AzeerDev): targets Saudi dialects specifically, 768-dim, license **Apache-2.0**, described as a v0.1 release. — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/AzeerDev/SA-STS-Embeddings-0.2B)
- **Swan / ArabicMTEB** (arXiv:2411.01192): a dialect-aware, Arabic-centric, cross-lingual/cross-cultural embedding model family plus its own benchmark (ArabicMTEB). Swan-Large is reported to be state-of-the-art on Arabic text embedding tasks, surpassing multilingual-e5-large on most Arabic tasks (self-reported). The paper states models "will be made publicly accessible for research" — license permissiveness (Apache/MIT vs. research-only) was **not confirmed**. — [arXiv:2411.01192](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.01192v2)

### Inferences
- For ARA's on-device RAG, **arabic-gte-multilingual-embed-medium** or **Arabic-Retrieval-v1.0** are the most phone-friendly Apache-2.0 options by size, but neither has demonstrated Egyptian-dialect-specific retrieval quality — both are effectively MSA-centric with unverified dialect robustness.
- **bahith-arabic-retrieval-4b** likely has the strongest general retrieval quality of the permissively-licensed options (built on a modern Qwen3-Embedding base) but at 4B is heavier than the BERT-based options and still MSA-focused by the card's own admission.
- The Swan/ArabicMTEB family appears to be the most rigorously dialect-aware embedding research, but its licensing is a blocker until confirmed — worth a direct follow-up check of its Hugging Face page/license file before considering it.

### Gaps
- No embedding model was found with **confirmed, benchmarked Egyptian-dialect retrieval performance** specifically (as distinct from MSA) under a clearly permissive license. This is a real gap for ARA to potentially fill or work around with query normalization/MSA-translation preprocessing.
- Swan's exact license terms were not retrieved in this session.
- No direct comparison of on-device inference speed/quantizability (e.g., ONNX, int8) was found for any of these embedding models — worth checking before integration.

## Key Question 3: Is there a standard/public benchmark for Egyptian Arabic dialect understanding specifically (not just MSA) that a project could use to validate its own Arabic quality claims?

### Takeaway
Yes — three relevant benchmarks explicitly cover Egyptian Arabic (AraDiCE, DialectalArabicMMLU, and Nile-Chat's own eval suite), with DialectalArabicMMLU being the newest, most rigorously human-curated, multi-dialect option, though its dataset license/availability for non-research use is unclear.

### Cited Findings
- **AraDiCE** (arXiv:2409.11404): a dialect- and culture-aware benchmark covering Gulf, Egyptian, and Levantine Arabic varieties plus a cultural-knowledge component; findings indicate LLMs handle dialect *understanding* better than dialect *generation* or *translation*. — [arXiv:2409.11404](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11404v3)
- **DialectalArabicMMLU** (arXiv:2510.27543, posted 31 Oct 2025): covers 5 dialects (Syrian, **Egyptian**, Emirati, Saudi, Moroccan) plus MSA and English; ~3,135 MMLU-Redux-v2 questions per dialect (~15K dialectal total, ~21,945 incl. MSA/English) across 32 academic/professional domains; built via **human translation** (translator/reviewer/adjudicator pipeline, not machine translation) with ~5% error rate found in spot-checking, leading to a revision round for UAE/Syrian subsets. The authors explicitly criticize ArabicMMLU's dialectal portion as "largely machine-translated and post-edited," positioning this as a more rigorous alternative. Stated as "intended exclusively for research and educational purposes" — commercial/product-validation use rights unclear. — [arXiv:2510.27543](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.27543v1)
- **Nile-Chat evaluation suite** (arXiv:2507.04569): includes Egyptian-script understanding tasks (MMLU, HellaSwag, PIQA) and generation/translation/transliteration tasks in both Arabic and Latin script; released alongside the Nile-Chat models as part of "all resources publicly available." — [arXiv:2507.04569](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04569v1)
- A **MADAR-based MT evaluation** (ACL Anthology 2026.abjadnlp-1.41) tests English↔dialectal-Arabic translation across 16 dialects including Egyptian, using BLEU, ChrF, COMET, and LLM-as-judge metrics — relevant if ARA needs translation-quality validation rather than just understanding. — [ACL 2026.abjadnlp-1.41](https://aclanthology.org/2026.abjadnlp-1.41.pdf)
- Broader/general Arabic leaderboards exist but are **not** dialect-specific: the **Open Arabic LLM Leaderboard 2** (published 10 Feb 2025) and **AraGen** (3C3H metric: Correctness, Completeness, Conciseness, Helpfulness, Honesty, Harmlessness; uses LLM-as-judge; runs 3-month blind test cycles, e.g. "AraGen-03-25") are the most-cited general Arabic generative benchmarks, but their dialect coverage was not confirmed. **Stanford HELM Arabic** (errata dated 2026-06-03, evaluating v1.0.0) did not state whether it separates MSA from dialectal performance. **BALSAM** is described as a newer community benchmarking platform for Arabic LLMs but was not explored in depth. — [AraGen 3C3H blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/leaderboard-3c3h-aragen); [Open Arabic LLM Leaderboard 2](https://huggingface.co/blog/leaderboard-arabic-v2); [BALSAM arXiv:2507.22603](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22603v1); [HELM Arabic](https://crfm.stanford.edu/2025/12/18/helm-arabic.html)
- A 2025 survey, "Evaluating Arabic Large Language Models: A Survey of Benchmarks, Methods, and Gaps" (arXiv:2510.13430), and a 2026 paper "Are Arabic Benchmarks Reliable? QIMMA's Quality-First Approach to LLM Evaluation" (arXiv:2604.03395) both signal active community concern about benchmark quality/reliability in Arabic NLP — relevant caveat for trusting any single leaderboard number at face value. — [arXiv:2510.13430](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.13430); [arXiv:2604.03395](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.03395)

### Inferences
- **DialectalArabicMMLU** is the strongest candidate for ARA to adopt as an external validation benchmark for Egyptian-dialect quality claims, given its human-translation methodology and explicit Egyptian subset — but ARA should confirm the dataset's actual license/terms before using it in any marketing claim or redistributing eval results, given the "research and educational purposes" framing.
- No benchmark found is Egyptian-*only*; all are multi-dialect benchmarks that include Egyptian as one of several varieties. If ARA wants an Egyptian-specific score to cite, it would need to filter these to the EGY subset and report that in isolation (as the Nile-Chat paper already does).

### Gaps
- Dataset license/download terms for DialectalArabicMMLU were not found (page did not state license or provide a confirmed download link).
- Whether AraGen, HELM Arabic, or BALSAM break out Egyptian-dialect-specific scores (vs. aggregate Arabic) was not confirmed — would require direct inspection of their leaderboard data tables.
- CamelBench/CAMEL-Bench (arXiv:2410.18976) was found but is a benchmark for **Arabic multimodal (LMM) models**, not dialect-focused text LLM evaluation — not directly relevant to ARA's text-focused on-device assistant; no active leaderboard for it was found.

## Key Question 4: Any notable funding, acquisition, or product news about Arabic-first AI startups or products that signals market demand or competitive risk?

### Takeaway
There is active but still early-stage/small-ticket funding activity specifically branded "Arabic-first AI" (Tarjama, Wittify.ai, Velents), alongside much larger multi-billion-dollar sovereign/regional AI infrastructure investment from Microsoft and Google tied to Gulf markets (not Arabic-NLP-specific but creating the ecosystem ARA competes/partners within); no major funding or product news was found for a direct on-device, privacy-first, Egyptian-dialect-specific assistant comparable to ARA, suggesting a possible open niche rather than crowded competition.

### Cited Findings

- **Velents (Egypt/Saudi Arabia)** raised **$1.5M** (Oct 2025), launching an "Arabic-speaking AI employee" product and planning an Arabic-first R&D center. — [EnterpriseAM](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2025/10/29/egyptian-saudi-ai-startup-velents-secures-usd-1-5-mn-in-funding/)

### Inferences
- The funding pattern shows Arabic-AI-branded startups are still raising small (sub-$2M to $15M) rounds — this is an early, unconsolidated market, not one with an obvious dominant incumbent in the specific on-device/privacy-first assistant niche ARA targets.
- The much larger capital ($10B+ each from Microsoft and Google) is flowing into *infrastructure* (cloud, sovereign AI hosting) rather than into consumer-facing Arabic-dialect assistants directly — this is enabling infrastructure for competitors but is not itself a competing product.
- No evidence was found of a well-funded, on-device-first, Egyptian-Arabic-specialized mobile assistant competitor — ARA's specific positioning (local-first, Egyptian-dialect-first, free) does not appear directly contested by any funded startup found in this search.
