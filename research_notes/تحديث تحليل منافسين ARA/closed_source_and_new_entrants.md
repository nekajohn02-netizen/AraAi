Research date: 2026-11-25. All "last checked" dates below are 2026-11-25 unless noted.

## Any new features, pricing changes, or app updates for Layla or Qandeel recently?

### Takeaway
Layla has shipped multiple real version updates (currently v7.8.0 on iOS App Store / v7.6.0–7.8.0 range on Android/APK) with concrete, dated release notes — MCP support, streaming-MoE, on-device image gen, and llama.cpp CLI-arg presets are now documented in the vendor's own changelog/App Store "What's New," which is a stronger evidence tier than the pure marketing-page claims found previously, though the app is still closed source so none of it is independently verified against a binary/source. Qandeel shows **no evidence of any update, feature, pricing, or documentation change** — its Play listing still cannot be rendered beyond the page title, and no changelog, blog, support page, or user reviews/forum discussion were found anywhere.

## Are there any NEW local-first Android AI assistant apps (open or closed), especially Arabic-first or major-player-backed ones?

### Takeaway
No major-player-backed (Google/Samsung/Huawei/Arabic-region tech giant) standalone *local-first on-device* Android assistant app was found. The most notable new Arabic-AI-chat entrant, Saudi Arabia's **HUMAIN Chat**, launched in 2025/2026 but is explicitly cloud-hosted (ALLAM 34B model running on HUMAIN's own Kingdom-based infrastructure, with real-time web search requiring connectivity) — it does **not** qualify as local-first and should be tracked as a cloud-based Arabic-AI competitor, not an on-device one. Among genuinely on-device apps, no new Arabic-first entrant was found; existing general-purpose local-LLM Android apps (MLC Chat, PocketPal AI / "Pocket LLM," Private AI, Maid) do not advertise Arabic-specific support.

### Cited Findings
- **HUMAIN Chat** (Saudi Arabia, developed by HUMAIN): publicly launched on web, iOS, and Android across Saudi Arabia; runs on **ALLAM 34B**, "fully hosted and operated in Saudi [Arabia]," supports real-time web search, Arabic speech input across dialects, and Arabic/English switching — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2613023), [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-launches-humain-chat-its-most-advanced-arabic-ai-1.500246219), checked 2026-11-25. This is a major-player-backed (Saudi state-linked HUMAIN) Arabic-first AI app, but it is cloud-hosted, not local-first — its web-search feature alone requires connectivity.
- **Fanar** (HBKU, Qatar): an Arabic-focused AI assistant supporting English, MSA, and Arabic dialects, text or speech — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/qa/app/fanar-%D9%81%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%B1/id6741857943), checked 2026-11-25. No information found on whether it runs on-device or is cloud-hosted; treat as a gap, not confirmed local-first.
- A PromptQuorum Arabic-language roundup article names six Android apps that "run LLMs fully offline in 2026": **MLC Chat, Maid, Layla, Ollama via Termux, Private AI, and PocketPal AI** — [promptquorum.com](https://www.promptquorum.com/ar/power-local-llm/best-local-llm-apps-android-2026), checked 2026-11-25. The article reports MLC Chat as the fastest of the group, but the fetched excerpt did not specify which of these apps handle Arabic input/output specifically.
- **Pocket LLM / "local-llms-on-android"** (open source, GitHub: dineshsoudagar): runs Qwen3 and Gemma 4 via LiteRT and ONNX Runtime; latest tracked release v1.5.0 adds voice input and theme options; 121 GitHub stars; no Arabic-specific claims found — [GitHub](https://github.com/dineshsoudagar/local-llms-on-android), checked 2026-11-25.
- A Hugging Face model, **Gemma-2B-Arabic-mobile**, is explicitly packaged/tuned for phone deployment (1555 MB file, reported ~11.4 tokens/sec on a Snapdragon 865 in a June 2026 test per the model card) — this is a model, not a packaged assistant app, but is a potential building block for an Arabic-first on-device app — [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/dispatchAI/Gemma-2B-Arabic-mobile), checked 2026-11-25 (the June-2026 benchmark claim itself is unverified independently).
- No results were found for a `github.com/topics/local-llm-android` topic page directly; adjacent/overlapping topics checked include `android-llm` and `on-device-ai`, surfacing mostly previously-known or non-Arabic projects (InferrLM, LlamaDroid, OlliteRT, "awesome-mobile-llm") — checked 2026-11-25.

### Inferences
- The Arabic-AI-assistant space is seeing real investment and major-player entry (HUMAIN Chat backed by the Saudi state/HUMAIN), but that investment is currently going into cloud-hosted apps, not local-first/on-device ones — this is a meaningful signal that ARA's "local-first + Arabic" combination remains relatively undefended even as the broader "Arabic AI assistant" category heats up.
- No credible new on-device, Arabic-first Android competitor emerged in the search; the competitive set for that specific niche still appears limited to Qandeel (unverifiable, possibly stale) plus general-purpose local-LLM apps that don't specifically target Arabic.

## Has Google's Gemini Nano/AICore or Samsung's on-device AI evolved in ways that matter for this market?

### Takeaway
Yes — Google is actively rolling out **Gemini Nano 4** (built on Gemma 4) as an AICore Developer Preview, with the preview setting reportedly reaching Pixel 11 devices around November 6, 2026; Samsung's Galaxy AI remains a hybrid on-device/cloud system (Gemini Nano + Samsung's own models for on-device features, cloud for others) with Samsung confirming in January 2026 that "basic" Galaxy AI features stay free indefinitely. Both are platform-level capabilities, not standalone assistant apps, but they raise the on-device-AI baseline that any Android user already has for free, which matters as context for ARA's positioning.

### Cited Findings
- **Gemini Nano 4**: previewed by Google in April 2026, built on Gemma 4, described as early access via "AICore Developer Preview" — [Android Developers blog](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/top-ai-on-android-updates-for-building-intelligent-experiences-from-google-i-o-26), [9to5google](https://9to5google.com/2026/04/02/gemini-nano-4/), checked 2026-11-25.
- A Japanese tech outlet (jetstream.blog) reports an "Enable AICore Developer Preview" toggle (off by default, under Developer Options > AICore Settings) began rolling out to Pixel 11 devices around **November 6, 2026**, with four switchable model variants currently available — [jetstream.blog](https://jetstream.blog/?p=228442), checked 2026-11-25. This is a single secondary-source report; no corroborating primary Google announcement with that exact date was found, so treat the specific date as Advertised/single-source, not confirmed.
- Google states AICore is distributed through Google Play (so model updates ship as ordinary app updates) and retains both old and new model data for up to 3 days post-update — [Google Support](https://support.google.com/android/answer/17065362), checked 2026-11-25.
- Samsung Galaxy AI uses "a hybrid processing architecture. Some features run entirely on-device using Gemini Nano and Samsung's own models. Others require cloud connectivity. Interpreter can run offline with downloaded language packs" — [fast.io Galaxy AI Review 2026](https://www.fast.io/resources/galaxy-ai-review-2026.md), checked 2026-11-25 (third-party review, not a Samsung primary source).
- Pre-launch reporting on the Exynos 2600 (the chip reportedly in Galaxy S26) claimed it would let Samsung "run larger AI models without internet access" — [GSMArena](https://www.gsmarena.com/galaxy_s26_will_deliver_a_big_ondevice_ai_upgrade-news-70894.php), checked 2026-11-25. This is pre-launch/rumor-tier, not confirmed shipping capability.
- Samsung confirmed (per third-party review, January 2026) that "basic Galaxy AI features remain free with no expiration date," while distinguishing "Samsung enhanced AI features" and "third-party AI features" which may carry fees — [fast.io](https://www.fast.io/resources/galaxy-ai-review-2026.md); Samsung's own Galaxy AI page corroborates that feature/model availability "may vary depending on the region/country, OS/One UI version, device model and phone carrier" — [samsung.com/galaxy-ai](https://www.samsung.com/galaxy-ai/), checked 2026-11-25.

### Inferences
- Google and Samsung are both pushing more capable on-device models into stock Android/Galaxy devices for free, which raises the bar for "why install a dedicated local-first assistant app" — but neither offers Arabic-dialect-tuned, fully-offline, swap-your-own-model flexibility the way Layla or a hypothetical ARA would, so the differentiation gap for a dedicated Arabic-first local app remains open even as the baseline improves.
- The Gemini Nano 4 rollout is still gated behind a developer-preview toggle on a single Pixel model as of the most recent report found, meaning for the mass market, AICore's practical capability level likely has not yet changed much; this is more a "coming soon" signal than a current competitive threat.

### Gaps
- No official Google changelog/blog post was found with an exact November 2026 AICore update date — only a single secondary (translated/aggregator) source reporting the November 6 rollout to Pixel 11. This should be verified against Google's own Android Developers blog or Pixel release notes if higher confidence is needed.
- No information was found on whether Gemini Nano or Samsung's on-device models currently support Arabic at a quality level relevant to competing with Arabic-first apps — this was not directly searched and is a notable gap given the project's Arabic-first focus.
